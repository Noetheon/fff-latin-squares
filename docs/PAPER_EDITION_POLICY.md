# Three Reading Editions

Maintain **three reading editions of one research project**, not three
independent papers claiming the same contribution. The editions share a named,
frozen evidence cutoff and inherit theorem and proof text from the same sources.
Editorial selection is not a new mathematical claim or external peer review.

| Edition | Reader and purpose | Size policy | Inclusion rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Compact** | A first mathematical reading or a manageable initial review | **12-18 total PDF pages, including front matter and references** | One coherent argument, complete prerequisites and proofs for the selected theorems, a few explicit examples, clear evidence limits |
| **Selected Results** | Readers examining a broader collection of constructions and structural results | No artificial page limit; currently 47 pages | Important established results with their dependencies; not every search campaign |
| **Full Research Report** | Detailed technical inspection and reproduction | No artificial page limit; currently 92 pages | Full proofs, finite computational reductions, dependency descriptions and precisely scoped research boundaries |

The previous 44-page "compact companion" becomes **Selected Results**. Its
mathematical content is retained. The new Compact is a focused selection, not
a second condensed technical report. The full report is not shortened.

## Future Updates

1. Freeze and identify the evidence intake before editing an edition. Do not
   import active-chat progress or unreviewed findings merely to make all PDFs
   appear current.
2. Correct errors consistently wherever the affected material occurs. Add a
   dated successor; do not overwrite frozen scientific sources or manifests.
3. Decide whether each new result belongs in Compact, Selected Results, the
   full report, or only the research record. New research does **not** require
   growth of Compact. A core correction can require updating all three; a
   local search result normally does not.
4. Reuse one theorem/proof source across editions. The current builder extracts
   complete, uniquely delimited source spans and records hashes. Do not
   maintain three manually diverging versions of the same argument.
5. Keep Compact at 12-18 **total** pages at normal 11-point text and readable
   margins. Exceeding the budget is a failed editorial gate. Remove secondary
   material or deliberately revise the scope; never hide missing proofs,
   shrink typography, or pad the document to meet the lower bound.
6. Rebuild all three when preparing an edition release. Check citations,
   references, theorem dependencies, status declarations, output hashes and
   actual rendered pages. Front matter and contents remain separated from
   the introduction. A successful compiler is not a visual review.
7. State which edition contains an imported computational theorem's complete
   argument. A reader must not mistake a summary for an independent proof.
8. Obtain explicit publication authorization before replacing public aliases.
   Publish edition labels, page counts, source links and hashes together; do
   not silently present overlapping editions as independent publications.

## Scientific and Publication Boundaries

All editions must retain the distinctions between written proofs, exact
computation, reconstructed evidence, and open questions. In the current
cutoff, the order-12 witness disproves C38 and unrestricted order 18 is open.
Higher-order represented-class lower bounds are not censuses.

Rights remain RESERVED. Authorship, venue requirements, complete durable
evidence delivery, novelty and independent expert review are separate gates.
Three carefully prepared reading editions do not resolve these gates.
