\begin{abstract}
For a pair of rows, columns, or symbols of a Latin square, the corresponding
two-line permutation is fixed-point-free.  We call a view failing if one of
these permutations has a nontrivial odd cycle, and call the square
\emph{FFF} if none of its three views fails.  Such odd cycles support local
Latin trades.  We prove the exact three-parity rule: row- and column-view odd
trades reverse the conventional row--column sign, while symbol-view odd
trades preserve it and reverse the two parastrophic companion signs.

We prove subsquare monotonicity and exact pattern preservation across
two-element congruence blocks; odd block size forces all three views to fail.
Hence every FFF quasigroup of order $2p$, for odd prime $p$, is
congruence-simple in every isotope.  We give an explicit non-group-isotopic
FFF loop of order $8$.  Direct products take the componentwise Boolean OR of
failure patterns and are group-isotopic exactly when both factors are,
producing infinite non-group-isotopic FFF families in power-of-two orders.
Binary line-rank defects correspond exactly to complementary four-packs of
half-order subsquares.  A direct specialization of known $3$-net character
theory also shows that one F view at order $2p$ forces a saturated integer
line-incidence lattice.

An exhaustive reproducible scan of the $283657$ order-$8$ main classes finds
$230$ FFF classes, of which $225$ are not group-isotopic; all eight failure
patterns occur.  No FFF square of order $6$ exists.  Consequently every order
$2^m$, $m\geq3$, realizes all eight patterns and contains a non-group-isotopic
FFF square.

At order $10$, we characterize an F-view by collision-free
odd-subset lifts and by total unimodularity of normalized pair-tensor slices.
The colour-stratified coboundary detects the full view pattern on the dual
flag surface.
Every normalized order-$10$ F-view generates $A_{10}$ or $S_{10}$, and at
least one view of a hypothetical FFF square generates $S_{10}$.  A complete
cycle-type palette audit proves that every view uses at least six of the seven
even derangement types by excluding all twenty-one exact five-type palettes.
Two exhaustive master searches then exclude all six-type palettes and the
seven-type palette.  Hence no FFF Latin square of order $10$ exists.  This
settles the second even non-power-of-two order after $6$, but the conjecture
that nontrivial FFF Latin squares exist exactly in power-of-two orders remains
open beginning at order $12$.
\end{abstract}
