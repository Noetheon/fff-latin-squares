\begin{abstract}
A Latin square is FFF when every permutation induced by two distinct rows,
columns, or symbols has only even cycles. This focused account proves that
the three-bit odd-cycle witness pattern of a direct product is the
componentwise Boolean OR of the factor patterns. A direct product is
group-isotopic precisely when both factors are group-isotopic. Odd orders
greater than one are excluded, while a group table is FFF exactly when the
group is a $2$-group. Pattern preservation through two-element congruence
blocks supplies an explicit non-group-isotopic FFF loop of order $8$ and,
by products, an infinite family. Separately labelled finite computations
record the small-order boundary and the order-$8$ census. An explicit
order-$12$ table disproves the former power-of-two-only conjecture.
The proofs, computational dependencies, and limits of these conclusions
are kept distinct. The unrestricted order-$18$ existence problem remains open.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Latin squares; quasigroups; direct products;
cycle structure; reproducible computation.\par
