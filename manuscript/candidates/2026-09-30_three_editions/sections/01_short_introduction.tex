\section{Introduction}

A Latin square has three interchangeable coordinate roles: row, column,
and symbol. Comparing two lines in one role gives a permutation of a
complementary coordinate set. The question considered here is whether any
such permutation has a nontrivial odd cycle. Recording the answer in all
three views gives an ordered pattern in $\{F,T\}^3$, where $T$ denotes the
presence of an odd-cycle witness. The designation FFF means that no view
has such a witness. Testing only rows, or only rows and columns, does not
establish this condition.

The central structural statements concern direct products. Their induced
permutations factor coordinatewise, and product-cycle lengths are least
common multiples of the factor lengths. This yields the exact rule
\[
 \pat(L\times K)=\pat(L)\mathbin{\mathrm{OR}}\pat(K).
\]
A separate closure criterion shows that the product is group-isotopic
if and only if both factors are. Both assertions include converses, and
neither relies on a finite census. In particular, an FFF factor preserves
the pattern of its partner, and a nongroup factor cannot become
group-isotopic by taking a direct product.

Two small examples make the scope concrete. A binary extension of the
cyclic group of order $4$ gives a nonassociative FFF loop of order $8$.
Its properties are proved, rather than inferred from an exhaustive scan.
At order $12$, an explicit table supplies a computationally checked FFF
example. Consequently, existence is not confined to powers of two, even
though every group-isotopic FFF square has power-of-two order.

For comparison, the finite data record contains no FFF square of order $6$
and exactly $230$ FFF main classes at order $8$, of which $225$ are not
group-isotopic. The census is relative to the external classification and
data of McKay, Meynert, and Myrvold~\cite{McKayMeynertMyrvold2007,McKayLatinData}.
These counts and the written proofs are different types of evidence.
The product construction gives higher-order existence, not injectivity on
main classes or a higher-order classification.

This paper is a focused reading edition of a larger research dossier.
It does not claim priority for standard permutation arguments, group-isotopy
criteria, or their formulations. The \emph{Selected Results} edition adds
further constructions and structural results; the \emph{Full Research
Report} supplies the detailed order-$10$ computational exclusion and
precisely bounded order-$18$ search results. Those dependencies are not
silently replaced by shorter arguments here.
