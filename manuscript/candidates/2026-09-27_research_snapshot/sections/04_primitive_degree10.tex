\section{A primitive-group reduction at order ten}
\label{sec:primitive-degree10}

Order $10$ admits a stronger reduction than congruence-simplicity.
Normalize the ten line permutations in one view at a base line, obtaining
\[
  H=\{h_0=1,h_1,\ldots,h_9\}\subseteq\operatorname{Sym}(10).
\]
Sharp transitivity is preserved, and the relative permutation between two
members is $h_j^{-1}h_i$.  Thus an F-view makes $H$ a clique of size ten in
the graph joining $g,h$ when $h^{-1}g$ has only even cycles.

\begin{computationaltheorem}[Primitive one-view reduction]
\label{cthm:primitive-view}
Every normalized F line family of degree $10$ is primitive.  Assuming the
complete standard classification of primitive permutation groups of degree
$10$, the group generated by that family is $A_{10}$ or $S_{10}$.
\end{computationaltheorem}

\begin{proof}
The only nontrivial equal-block partitions of ten points have two blocks of
size five or five blocks of size two.  The first case is excluded by a direct
odd-block argument.

For the second case, write the points as $(i,t)$ with $i\in\{0,\ldots,4\}$
and $t\in\mathbb F_2$.  Every block-preserving member $g$ has the form
\[
 g(i,t)=(\phi_g(i),t+f_g(i)).
\]
The projection $g\mapsto\phi_g$ is injective on the normalized F-family.
Indeed, if distinct $g,h$ had the same projection, then $h^{-1}g$ would fix
each two-point block setwise.  Since it is fixed-point-free, it flips every
block.  Choose a third family member $k$.  The two relative maps
$k^{-1}g$ and $k^{-1}h$ induce the same permutation $\psi$ of the five
blocks.  The odd set of blocks forces $\psi$ to have an odd cycle $D$,
possibly a fixed point.  The binary fibre voltages of the two relative maps
around $D$ differ by $|D|\equiv1\pmod2$.  Exactly one voltage is therefore
zero, and its lift consists of two odd cycles of length $|D|$ (fixed points
when $|D|=1$), contradicting the F-condition.

Sharp transitivity now makes the ten distinct projections a two-fold sharply
transitive subset of $S_5$, with binary fibre voltages.  The resulting exact
finite formula is unsatisfiable; its DRAT proof has been independently
checked.  Hence the generated group is primitive.

The primitive-group classification~\cite{BuekenhoutLeemans1996,GAPPrimGrp}
lists nine degree-$10$ actions.  A frozen GAP 4.16.0/PrimGrp 4.0.3 query
reproduces the catalogue list and versions the external classification
boundary.  The actions have orders
\[
  60,120,360,720,720,720,1440,|A_{10}|,|S_{10}|.
\]
The first seven are the actions of $A_5,S_5$ on $2$-subsets and the natural
projective-line actions of
$\operatorname{PSL}(2,9)$, $\operatorname{PGL}(2,9)$,
$\operatorname{P\Sigma L}(2,9)\cong S_6$, $M_{10}$, and
$\operatorname{P\Gamma L}(2,9)$; the extension notation agrees with the
ATLAS~\cite{AtlasA6}.  Direct construction verifies their orders, closure,
transitivity, and primitivity.  The exact maximum sizes of F-cliques including
the identity are, in the same order,
\[
  1,2,1,2,2,2,4.
\]
Two bounds follow from complete enumeration with no eligible nonidentity
element.  Each of the other five upper bounds is an UNSAT CNF with an
independently checked DRAT trace.  None can contain the required clique of
size ten, so only $A_{10}$ and $S_{10}$ remain.
\end{proof}

The three views are further coupled by signs.  If $k_R,k_C,k_S$ count the odd
row-, column-, and symbol-line permutations, then
\begin{equation}
  k_R+k_C+k_S\equiv 1\pmod 2.
  \label{eq:three-view-sign}
\end{equation}
Indeed, on the $n^2$ cells the row-entry, column-entry, and symbol-position
bijections differ by coordinate transposition.  Taking signs gives
\[
  R_{\rm sign}C_{\rm sign}S_{\rm sign}
  =(-1)^{n(n-1)/2},
\]
which is negative at $n=10$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:at-least-one-s10}
In every hypothetical FFF Latin square of order $10$, the normalized
generated group in each view is $A_{10}$ or $S_{10}$, and at least one view
generates $S_{10}$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
If a normalized view group lies in $A_{10}$, all unnormalized line
permutations have the same sign.  Its number of odd lines is therefore $0$ or
$10$, hence even.  If all three view groups were $A_{10}$,
Equation~\eqref{eq:three-view-sign} would fail.  Computational
Theorem~\ref{cthm:primitive-view} leaves $S_{10}$ as the only alternative.
\end{proof}

This result by itself excludes neither remaining group and therefore does not
settle order $10$; Computational
Theorem~\ref{cthm:order10-nonexistence} does so later by a different exhaustive
argument.  The primitive-group reduction replaces an unrestricted primitive
search by a three-view compatibility problem inside $A_{10}$ and $S_{10}$.
As a bounded diagnostic, all $135$ distinct valid order-$10$ partial tables
already frozen in the computational corpus were audited independently.  Of
their $257$ F-views, $256$ generate $S_{10}$ and one generates $A_{10}$;
$93$ of the non-FFF tables already generate $S_{10}$ in all three views.
Thus generated-group order alone does not separate FFF squares from current
near-models.  These figures describe only the frozen corpus and are not a
census of order-$10$ Latin squares.
