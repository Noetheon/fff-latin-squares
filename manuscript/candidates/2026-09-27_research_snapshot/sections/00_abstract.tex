\begin{abstract}
A Latin square is FFF when every two-line permutation in each of its
row, column and symbol views has only even cycles. Direct products take
the componentwise Boolean OR of the three failure patterns and are
group-isotopic exactly when both factors are. Subsquare and congruence
obstructions restrict possible constructions. Exact small-order checks
exclude orders $6$ and $10$, while independently checked tables of orders
$12$ and $14$ refute the former power-of-two existence conjecture.
Finite-field constructions give additional infinite even-order families.
An affine prime-successor family has an exact three-view criterion
and parameter classification; a mixed-character-sum estimate gives
more than $p/6000$ represented FFF classes at order $p+1$ for
every prime $p\geq10^{12}$. This does not determine the complete
order spectrum.

A row-dependent extension has pattern equal to the OR of its quotient
and fibre patterns. When its fibres have no binary three-sorted quotient,
all binary quotients descend from the outer square. For an elementary
abelian $2$-group as outer square, this recovers the layers intrinsically and classifies
a fixed family of fibre assignments by affine orbits. A finite audit
supplies $514$ pairwise distinct FFF main classes of order $20$; the
construction and Burnside's lemma then give explicit lower bounds in
orders $20\cdot2^d$, not censuses of those orders. The order-$20$ quotient
freeness uses the computational order-$10$ exclusion.

An exact position--value coloring formulation supplies a smaller
three-view constraint representation with automatic color balance.
Proofs, finite computations, and bounded search outcomes are distinguished
throughout. Existence at order $18$, the complete spectrum, and literature
priority for the recent constructions remain unresolved.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Latin square; quasigroup; Latin trade; direct
product; main class; affine orbit; computational enumeration.
